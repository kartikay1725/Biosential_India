# BioSentinel India — Critical Limitations & Epistemic Boundaries

This document defines the formal operational limitations, methodological boundaries, and proper interpretations of BioSentinel India. Every reviewer, researcher, and practitioner interacting with this system must adhere to these caveats.

---

## 1. Occurrence Counts vs. Ecological Abundance

> [!WARNING]
> **Occurrence records reflect human observation effort, NOT biological population abundance.**

- GBIF and iNaturalist data are generated by opportunistically sampled citizen science contributions, ornithological excursions, and academic field surveys.
- A sudden increase in occurrences in a grid cell generally reflects increased observer presence, better cellular connectivity, app adoption, or weekend birding events—**not** an influx of wildlife.
- Conversely, a drop in occurrences frequently reflects bad weather, restricted access, observer churn, or holiday periods—**not** a wildlife die-off.
- BioSentinel attempts to normalize for this using log-effort predictors and dual-slope residuals, but unmeasured sampling biases (e.g., changes in observer skill, gear, or target species preference) cannot be fully eliminated.

---

## 2. Anomalies vs. Ecological Ground Truth

> [!CAUTION]
> **A statistical anomaly is NOT proof of ecological change, biodiversity loss, or species recovery.**

- Statistical anomalies indicate that the observed species richness or composition departs from the conditional model expectation.
- Reasons for departures include:
  1. **Sampling Discontinuities:** A new observer recording cryptic species never previously logged.
  2. **Taxonomic Drift:** Nomenclature updates or splitting/lumping of species keys.
  3. **Spatial Clustering within Cells:** Observations concentrated in a single protected park within a 25km grid.
  4. **True Ecological Shifts:** Habitat alteration, drought, fire, or climatic events.
- BioSentinel flags **where to look**, not **what occurred**. Ground-truthed ecological surveys are mandatory before any policy or management action.

---

## 3. Absence of Labelled Anomaly Ground Truth

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **No anomaly classification accuracy, precision, or recall is claimed, because true ecological anomalies are unlabelled at the subcontinent scale.**

- In the absence of an exhaustive, verified registry of historical biodiversity collapse events across India, unsupervised and semi-supervised anomaly detection cannot report supervised metrics (e.g., ROC-AUC or true classification accuracy) for anomaly labels.
- Evaluation is therefore grounded on:
  - Walk-forward predictive errors ($\text{MAE}$, $\text{RMSE}$) of conditional expected baselines.
  - Goodness-of-fit likelihoods ($\text{AIC}$, dispersion parameters).
  - Multi-method consensus agreements.
  - Rank-order sensitivity stability ($\text{Spearman } \rho$).

---

## 4. Priority Scores are Screening Signals, Not Severity Scores

> [!NOTE]
> **Priority scores are resource-allocation indices for survey teams, not measures of ecosystem destruction.**

- The formula:
  $$\text{Priority Score} = \text{Evidence Score} \times \left(\frac{\text{Reliability Score}}{100}\right)$$
  is intentionally engineered to downweight under-sampled areas.
- A low priority score does **not** mean an ecosystem is healthy; it may mean the area is severely data-deficient and cannot support reliable statistical inference.
- Priority scores do not establish causation, ecological damage, or extinction debt.

---

## 5. Spatial Granularity & Coordinate Precision

- Grid cells are defined using Equal-Area Cylindrical Projection (EQDGC) at 0.25° spatial resolution (~25km × 25km).
- All reported coordinates (latitude, longitude) represent the **geometric centroid** of the 0.25° grid unit.
- They must **never** be cited as the precise GPS location of an individual animal observation.

---

## 6. BioCLIP Vision Model Scope & Confidence Boundaries

- The BioCLIP partial fine-tuned model (ViT-B/16) achieves **78.94% Top-1** and **92.87% Top-5 accuracy** across 1,106 Indian species.
- **Model Confidence:** Confidence scores and softmax probabilities apply **strictly** when an actual photographic image is evaluated through the vision pipeline.
- Confidence scores are **never** fabricated, imputed, or back-filled for historical GBIF occurrence records that lack evaluated photographic images.
- BioCLIP is an observation and species identification intelligence tool; it does not replace the validated GBIF taxonomic backbone.
